The Cost of Keeping AI Data in Nepal

Every time I look at a cheap inference API, my rented GPUs start looking like an expensive hobby. Then I remember I’m building fintech in Nepal, where “where does the data go?” belongs in the same spreadsheet as “what does it cost?” The boundary I design for is sensitive customer data staying inside Nepal’s borders, including during AI processing, unless there is an approved basis to do otherwise. NRB requirements and the institution’s approvals help determine which options I can actually use. “Zero retention” does not make a foreign server Nepali. Geography has declined our request for a workaround.

So this is the small comedy I get to manage every day: make the chatbot useful, make the infrastructure cheaper, and keep customer data within the boundary we have to respect. A rented GPU can spend the afternoon doing absolutely nothing and still cost exactly the same. Keeping it for another project does not make its bill disappear, and saying “compliance” does not turn idle capacity into good engineering. I still have to measure the work, improve the setup, and explain the spend. The model may hallucinate. The invoice does not.
